# Creating Digital Case Studies — Workshop Plan

## Overview
**Duration:** 90 minutes  
**Format:** 50% delivery / 50% activity  
**Audience:** Beginners (limited experience with digital tools)

## Intended Outcomes
By the end of the session, students will:
- Understand the assessment requirements
- Know what AI use is allowed vs not allowed
- Have chosen a topic
- Have defined a target audience
- Understand a simple structure for their case study

---

## Session Plan

### 0–10 min — Opening (Example First)

**Activity:**
- Show a short example digital case study (video / infographic-style)

**Prompt students:**
- Who is this aimed at?
- What made it easy or difficult to understand?

**Key message:**
> Clarity matters more than polish.

---

### 10–25 min — Assignment Overview

**Cover:**
- 3-minute digital case study
- 500-word reflective commentary
- AutoViva transcript

**Success looks like:**
- Clear message
- Defined audience
- Logical structure
- Evidence-informed content

---

### AI Use (Explicit Guidance)

**Allowed:**
- Brainstorming ideas
- Drafting scripts
- Generating visuals

**Not allowed:**
- Writing the reflection
- Fake or shallow justification
- Substituting understanding

**Key message:**
> Over-reliance on AI becomes obvious in reflection and AutoViva.

---

### 25–35 min — Platforms

**Examples:**
- Canva
- CapCut
- PowerPoint
- iMovie

**Key message:**
> The tool does not matter. Clarity does.

**Reassure:**
- Watermarks are fine
- Free tools are fine
- No marks for polish alone

---

### 35–50 min — Structure

## The 4-Part Flow

1. Hook — Why should I care?
2. Explain — What is the issue?
3. Solution — What can be done?
4. Takeaway — What should the audience think or do?

**Key message:**
> Structure is your safety net.

---

### Mini Task (2–3 min)

**Example topic:** Climate change and food security

Students think:
- What is the hook?
- What is the takeaway?

---

### 50–70 min — Student Activity

**Individual work:**
- Choose a topic (from handbook)
- Define a target audience (specific, non-technical)
- Sketch:
  - Hook idea
  - Main message

---

### Pair Activity (5–7 min)

Students explain to a partner:
- Their topic
- Their audience
- Their idea

Partner asks:
> Would I understand this?

---

### 70–80 min — Common Pitfalls

**Highlight:**

- No audience  
  → “General public” is usually too vague  

- No structure  
  → Feels like disconnected facts  

- Overuse of AI  
  → Sounds polished but lacks depth  

---

### 80–90 min — Close

**Recap:**
- You do not need advanced tools
- You do need:
  - A clear audience
  - A clear structure
  - A clear message

**Final message:**
> If you can explain your idea clearly to a non-expert, you are most of the way there.

---

## Notes

- Focus on clarity, not complexity
- Avoid over-teaching tools
- Prioritise momentum over perfection
